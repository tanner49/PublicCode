# Halftime-aware blowout experiment: 2025 frozen holdout

Production ratings are unchanged. Only the Games to Watch graphic filter was changed on the website.

## Design

Train on the actual archived Week 10 export, using only completed 2025 regular-season games through Week 10 and kickoffs before November 5, 2025 00:00 UTC. Freeze those team ratings and predict all available Weeks 11-14 games without any updating. The later export provides evaluation scores only. Training: 2,855 games, including 516 FBS-vs-FBS. Evaluation: 844 games, including 236 FBS-vs-FBS and six FBS-vs-FCS. No evaluated games had an unrated team. No Week 15/postseason results are available as scored holdout games.

Quarter scores are usable only when at least four nonnegative periods sum exactly to the final score for both teams. Valid for 2,834/2,855 training games, including all 516 FBS-vs-FBS games. Otherwise use the current capped target. No need to fall back to 2026.

All candidates retain the existing 2.75 winner bonus, signed targets, game-count weights, prior transformation and sum-to-zero constraint. Primary prior weight 1.0; fixed sensitivity 0.75. Only archived 2024 priors are used. All forecast variants receive +3 home advantage except neutral games. Baseline matrix coefficients were verified against the production solver to 1e-8. This recreates the current model family, not the historical notebook's tiny prior weight.

## Rules fixed before reading these holdout results

- Baseline: final-margin credit capped at 28.
- Halftime variants: only if the eventual winner led by at least 21 at halftime and still won by at least 10, use the larger of min(final winning margin, 28) and multiplier times halftime lead, with the halftime-based credit capped at 56. Multipliers: 1.0, 1.25, 1.5. Otherwise use baseline.
- Soft-cap comparison: full credit through 28, then 25% credit for excess final margin, capped at 56.
- Uncapped control: final margin with no cap, retaining the same win bonus and other fitting details. This is a control, not a proposed production replacement.

Example for 1.25x: halftime lead 14 and final win 35 gets 28 margin credit; halftime lead 28 and final win 35 gets 35; halftime lead 42 and final win 49 gets 52.5; an extreme halftime lead stops at 56 credit. Add the 2.75 bonus afterward. A near-collapse to a final one-point win gets ordinary one-point credit, not the halftime uplift. The rule is symmetric for home and away.

## FBS-vs-FBS holdout results (236 games; primary prior 1.0)

| Model | Final-margin MAE | Capped-margin MAE | Winners correct | MAE change vs baseline |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cap28 | 12.353 | 10.692 | 171/236 | +0.000 |
| half1.0 | 12.256 | 10.594 | 170/236 | -0.097 |
| half1.25 | 12.175 | 10.514 | 174/236 | -0.178 |
| half1.5 | 12.129 | 10.459 | 174/236 | -0.224 |
| softcap25pct | 12.260 | 10.593 | 170/236 | -0.093 |
| uncapped | 12.154 | 10.501 | 170/236 | -0.199 |

Capped-margin MAE clips both the prediction and the actual final margin to +/-28 before taking absolute error. It reduces the influence of exact blowout size; winner accuracy directly checks whether ordering improved on these played matchups. Neither proves a globally superior ranking.

The 1.25x version improves winner picks by three net (five corrections, two newly wrong picks). It changes seven winner predictions, several extremely close to zero. It widens 52 of the 56 FBS-vs-FBS forecasts whose baseline absolute spread was at least 14, narrowing four. This addresses underdog lean, but is primarily widening expected mismatches rather than demonstrating a refined ATS selection advantage.

For 1.25x, paired final-margin MAE improves by 0.178 points; a whole-week bootstrap interval is [-0.455, +0.099]. For 1.5x, improvement is 0.224, interval [-0.598, +0.159]. Both include no improvement. Only four holdout weeks exist, with repeated teams; these intervals are rough descriptive uncertainty measures.

## Sensitivity and limitations

At prior 0.75, baseline MAE is 12.294 with 171 correct winners; 1.25x gives 12.133 and 174, while 1.5x gives 12.105 and 174. The direction survives the prior change. Six FBS-vs-FCS holdout games are far too few to validate the motivating cross-division hypothesis. Across all 844 games, 1.25x improves MAE 13.447 to 13.016 and winners 639 to 648; uncapped has the lowest all-division MAE (12.717), so broader claims that capping always helps are unsupported.

The quarter rule changes 597 training targets at 1.25x, including 54 FBS-vs-FBS and 52 FBS-vs-FCS. The remaining changed games are lower-division contests in the same fitted network. Inputs and changes are recorded in the CSV audit.

No historical ATS conclusion: archive/2025/cfb_betting_lines.csv was generated by cbd.ipynb from the model and read as pred_lines in results.ipynb; it is not an independent sportsbook closing-lines dataset. This evaluates scores and winners, not profitability or bookmaker-relative picks.

This specific rule family was fixed before evaluating the holdout in this run. The 2025 data had been used in a previous Q3 experiment, so it is not a pristine never-examined season. Comparing multiple candidates and selecting a favorite is exploratory; do not tune further on these holdout games. The earlier Q3 experiment merely restored the same 28 cap and barely affected results. This experiment allows carefully limited credit beyond that cap.

## Recommendation

Keep 1.25x as a conservative side-model candidate for prospective comparison; 1.5x is slightly better on this holdout but not compellingly so. Do not change production based on these small differences. Record future predictions before games, then compare capped-margin error, winner picks, and ATS where independent closing lines exist.
